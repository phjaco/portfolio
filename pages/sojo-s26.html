SOJO Internship

I'm opting to omit any photos of my internship beyond very generic sketches because I'm fairly confident that an amount of my work was proprietary to what the company and I don't have the authority to share it. This also means I'll keep this section brief, because without the visual additive it's less interesting to discuss.

Case Traceability

The case traceability project had the goal of individually applying an identifier to every case that is processed on a line, so that when it leaves the facility each case can be backtraced through the packaging system back to the original input manufacturer. I designed the entire mechanical housing assembly while balancing suppliers for different components to minimize costs. I created the layout of the electrical cabinet attached to the assembly in CAD and built the entire electrical cabinet under supervision from the lead cabinet assembler. When I left the internship, the project was nearly complete, and had entered the initial testing of the code. There was a hardware issue with a component beyond our control that prevented it from being completed the week that I left, but overall the project was successfully completed on my end.

Additional Projects

I served several other roles during my internship as well. I created CAD models and technical drawings for replacement parts needed to get pallet positioners and robotic end effectors back operational. I created two or three unique adjustable mandrel designs for trayformers, in addition to two generic parametric designs to easily create static mandrel technical drawings once I left. I created video instructions for how to change those parametric mandrels to the desired dimensions. During my time, there was a staff transition that left the responsibilities of the only on-site mechanical engineer open for a few weeks, and during that time I attempted to fill those responsibilities by helping to overhaul some of the projects they were working on. This included learning sheet metal modeling from scratch and overhauling an entire sheet metal assembly that had been spec'd too thin, then redesigning the entire thing in an 11 hour shift on my final day. There was also continual management of 6 axis robotic arms used in palletizing inputted trays, as well as dismantling for transport roboticized lines at their previous facilities. I am likely forgetting additional things I worked on, but I worked over time to ensure I completed every task I could.
