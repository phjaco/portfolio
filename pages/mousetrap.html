Mousetrap Car

This was a project for a competition in our Intro to Mechanical Engineering course. It was a group project and I took responsibility for designing the car, its construction, and troubleshooting any issues. I worked with another group's designer to figure the optimal lever length to wheel size ratio, then I took that number and altered it by 5% under the assumption there'd be unaccounted for losses in the math. A group of students on the Motorsports team got in on a bearing order, so I had four bearings to work with, and we used .125" aluminum shafts with PLA printed bodies and wheels. The spring mechanism was removed from the mousetrap and a new lever was bent to fit the intended shape. The biggest issue in troubleshooting was that the car would not go straight, which took several hours of trouble shooting to figure out. I added a plastic bonder for tread on the wheels, aligned the bearings with the housing so they'd sit symmetrically, created spacers so the wheels would be aligned. Ultimately, the issue was the axle was moving too quickly and slipping in the pressfit wheels, which was fixed with superglue. The team that was collaborated with for the math was told of this issue.

Here's four of the designs we went through; the first one is a block, the second one introduced a levered design, the third extends that intended length, the fourth removed the mousetrap base and just used the spring from it, and the final version below transitioned it into a V shape with larger bearing housings. I didn't have an understanding of the math behind tearout at the time, but I did have inklings of the concept. The bulbous ends on the rear wheels were intended to limit the surface area that could contact the wheels; this wasn't an issue as they never crept in, and spacers were added to ensure they never did so.

Final Version.

Our team won the entire tournament, which is why I included it as a project in the portfolio.
