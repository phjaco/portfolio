Clock Geartrain

The clock geartrain was a project assigned in one of our courses that involved solving loading equations based on an inputted torque and weight of a clock's hands to determine the minimum acceptable values in designing a clock geartrain, and then actually creating the drawing for it and sourcing additional parts rated to or above the values we determined. This boiled out to solving the alternating and constant bending forces on two shafts as the hands rotate, finding the bearing stresses acting on the points along the shaft that we placed them, and creating gears that both don't break under the load and have a functional ratio.

This project required ~40 hours of work from me, 18 of which were continuous from 5AM to 11AM the night of the deadline. I was responsible for all 3D models and technical drawings, which changed constantly as last minute tweaks were made. The total report was 52 pages long and I was directly responsible for half of them, and reviewed and edited the rest to check both the work and wording.

The report is not perfect, and AI was used to create scripts that captured the MathScript from our work in Desmos, translated it into LateX to be compiled in Overwolf and exported as a pdf, and then the collection of pdfs was combined using an adjustable pdf combiner that was also an AI generated tool. This was disclosed to our instructor and no AI was used in reaching our actual conclusions. The reason we did this was because our group had a slight contribution imbalance such that three of the four categories of calculations were still being worked on the day prior to the due date, with a 6+ hour call between I and another groupmate that ran well into the night. Our sleep schedules were coordinated such that I left the call early and woke up within an hour of being sent the finalized numbers of importance to begin the drawings. The LateX calculations were edited quickly, but lack well defined variables and weren't compressed down or simplified, only labeled and numbered accordingly.

Given the larger workload required by this project and the circumstances, this is one of the course projects I am proudest of despite its flaws.
